# Payment policy and approval engine

The policy engine is deterministic; it never consults an LLM. A decision is a policy assessment, not payment authorization. `ELIGIBLE_FOR_FUTURE_AUTOPAY` means only that the configured policy checks pass for a future, separately implemented workflow. This service does not initiate, schedule, or execute payments and does not call Plaid, Stripe, ACH, or any other money-movement provider. Approval is not payment.

All policy, evaluation, and approval endpoints require a Firebase bearer token and scope database reads/writes by the verified UID. Policy bank-connection IDs are owner-checked; account last four is only a comparison reference, never proof of ownership or authorization. The database stores no payment account number or bank credential in policy or decision snapshots. Approval responses omit token hashes.

An evaluation stores a fixed policy snapshot and canonical bill snapshot/hash. `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` creates a single pending approval bound to that snapshot. Its random token is hashed before storage, is never logged or returned, and is reserved for a future trusted notification flow; current approve/reject/cancel actions require Firebase authentication. Approval expires after `PAYMENT_APPROVAL_TTL_HOURS` (1-720 hours; defaults to 24). The unique pending-approval index, bill row lock, and snapshot comparisons prevent duplicate active approvals and approving stale bill details. Changed bill data cancels a stale approval at evaluation or attempted action. Approval never changes the bill to paid, and this service never sets `CONSUMED`.

Policies with missing or ambiguous required information result in manual review or explicit approval. The default first-payment, amount-change, and due-date-change settings require approval. Since this repository has no payment execution/history workflow, an approval cannot prove that a first payment occurred; first-payment approval therefore remains required until a future system records a consumed payment safely. Configure policies and approval expiration explicitly in production, apply database migrations before deploying functions, and retain audit logs without adding secrets or token material.
